*The simulator's stress system has two layers with very different time horizons. Acute stress (adrenaline + glucagon, t½ ≈ 60 min, capped at 0.4 in T1D) responds to hypoglycemia and to anaerobic exercise; HAAF blunts this response when repeated hypos accumulate. Chronic stress (cortisol, t½ ≈ 12 h) builds up from sleep loss and illness. Both feed a unified stressMultiplier together with circadian cortisol, protein-glucagon, and the glycogen-dependent baseline; the liver computes EGP = EGP₀ · max(0, stressMultiplier − x3). Acute stress also draws on the finite hepatic glycogen pool, which explains why prolonged counterregulation gradually weakens. **acuteStress**: rapid catecholamine + glucagon response — driven by hypo (Somogyi) and intense exercise; **chronicStress**: sustained cortisol — driven by sleep loss (+0.06 per lost hour) and illness; **glycogenReserve (R)**: 0–1 fraction of the 90 g hepatic glycogen pool; **glycogenBaseline** = 0.5 · R; **gngBaseline** = 0.5 + 0.25 · (1 − R) — compensatory GNG (Petersen 2004); **effectiveAcuteStress** = acuteStress · (0.6 · R + 0.4) — glycogen-permissive 60/40 split; **counterRegFactor** (HAAF gate): 0.3 + 0.7 · exp(−hypoArea / 30) — see HAAF figure; **circadianCortisol**: dawn phenomenon contribution (sine, peak 08:00, amplitude ≈ 0.15); **protein-glucagon**: AA-Hill response from the protein-glucagon pathway; T1D cap: total acuteStress is clamped at 0.4 vs. ~5.0 in healthy individuals (loss of glucagon); **EGP₀**: basal hepatic production (~0.0161 × weight mmol/min for T1D); **x3**: insulin's EGP-suppression effect — opposes the stressMultiplier.*
