Stress hormone system — two-layer counterregulation (Cryer 2013)

Simulator implementation of two-tier glucose counterregulation as described in Cryer 2013 (Cryer PE, J Clin Invest 123:1403-1405, 2013). Layer 1 — acute: catecholamine- and glucagon-driven glycogenolysis (t½ ≈ 60 min, T1D cap 0.4 vs ~5.0 in healthy individuals due to absent glucagon response; Cryer 2013 Table 1). Layer 2 — chronic: cortisol-mediated gluconeogenesis upregulation from sleep loss and illness (t½ ≈ 12 h). A HAAF gate (Hypoglycemia-Associated Autonomic Failure) attenuates acuteStress when repeated hypoglycemia accumulates, modeled after the Cryer 2013 hierarchy. Both layers converge with circadian cortisol, protein-glucagon, and the glycogen-dependent baseline onto a unified stressMultiplier; the liver then computes EGP = EGP0 · max(0, stressMultiplier − x3). Acute stress simultaneously depletes the hepatic glycogen pool, so prolonged counterregulation raises GNG compensation while glycogenolysis capacity falls.
