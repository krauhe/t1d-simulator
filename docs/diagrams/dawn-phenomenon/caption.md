*The simulator splits the morning BG rise into two coupled mechanisms. Mechanism 1 — HGP: a sine-arc circadianCortisol curve (peak 08:00, amplitude 0.15) is added to the stressMultiplier so the liver produces more glucose. Mechanism 2 — peripheral resistance: circadianISF varies between 0.70 (morning) and 1.20 (evening) and rescales kb1/kb2/kb3, so the same plasma insulin produces less effect at dawn. The amplitude is regenerated at midnight as 0.15 · (1 + 0.12·h_lost) · (1 + 0.30·chronicStress), letting poor sleep and lingering chronic stress amplify the next morning's effect. **t**: time-of-day in 24-hour clock; **h_lost**: hours of sleep lost (clamped 0–4) — converted to chronicStress at 07:00; **chronicStress**: residual cortisol from yesterday (t½ ≈ 12 h); dawn amplitude regen at day change: baseAmp · (1 + 0.12·h_lost) · (1 + 0.30·chronic) — clamped 0.05–0.35; **circadianCortisol**: symmetric sine arc, rises 4 h before peak and falls 4 h after; **circadianISF**: cosine interpolation between control points — 1.20 (00–04), 0.70 (08:00), 1.00 (14:00), 1.20 (19–24); combined morning effect at 08:00 ≈ 1.15 / 0.70 ≈ 1.64 → ~64 % more insulin needed; EGP = EGP₀ · max(0, stressMultiplier − x3) — circadianCortisol enters via stressMultiplier; currentISF = (baseISF · circadianISF · vasodilatation · postExBoost) / (insulinResist · ffaResist) — kb_i scaled each tick.*
