HAAF — area-based hypo memory and counterregulation gate (Cryer / Dagogo-Jack)

Hypoglycemia-Associated Autonomic Failure (HAAF; Cryer 1992, Cryer et al. 2003) is modelled as an area-based memory rather than a discrete episode count. When BG falls below 3.0 mmol/L, hypoArea accumulates proportionally to depth × duration; when BG stays above 4.0 mmol/L it decays with t½ ≈ 3 simulated days (calibrated to Dagogo-Jack et al., J Clin Invest 91:819-828, 1993 and Cranston et al., Lancet 344:283-287, 1994). The accumulated burden passes through a sigmoid that produces counterRegFactor between 0.3 and 1.0. This factor multiplies the acute stress amplitude — modelled here as the gate factor multiplying acute stress amplitude in the stress multiplier — so repeated hypoglycemia progressively blunts the catecholamine/glucagon rescue response. Acute stress in T1D is already capped at 0.4 (vs ~5.0 healthy) due to absent glucagon secretion; HAAF further reduces what little counter-regulatory reserve remains.
