*Hypoglycemia-Associated Autonomic Failure (HAAF) is modelled as an area-based memory rather than a discrete episode count. When BG falls below 3.0 mmol/L, hypoArea accumulates proportionally to depth × duration; when BG stays above 4.0 it decays with t½ ≈ 3 simulated days (Dagogo-Jack 1993, Cranston 1994). The accumulated burden passes through a sigmoid that produces counterRegFactor between 0.3 and 1.0. This factor multiplies the acute stress amplitude, so repeated hypos progressively blunt the catecholamine/glucagon rescue response — the simulator's representation of the clinical vicious cycle. **hypoArea**: cumulative hypo burden (mmol·min equivalent); accumulates at 3.0 − BG when BG < 3.0; hypo decay: exponential half-life ≈ 3 simulated days (recovery when BG > 4.0); **counterRegFactor**: 0.3 + 0.7 · exp(−hypoArea / 30) — floor at 0.3 (HAAF never fully erases response). Calibration: BG 2.5 for 20 min ≈ 20 % reduction; two same-day hypos ≈ 40–50 % reduction; 3 hypo-free sim-days ≈ near-full recovery. Effect: acute stress amplitude is multiplied by counterRegFactor when written into the stress multiplier. Acute stress in T1D is already capped at 0.4 (vs ~5.0 healthy) due to lost glucagon — HAAF further reduces what little remains.*
