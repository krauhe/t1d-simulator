*Protein raises BG in T1D through a glucagon-driven, hepatic pathway. A three-compartment absorption model (proteinStomach → proteinGut → aminoAcidsBlood, sharing τG with carbs) feeds a Hill response that drives the stress multiplier. The glucagon effect splits into glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis: it is full when liver glycogen is replete and falls to ~50 % when glycogen is depleted (only the GNG half remains). The output is added to the unified stressMultiplier and is added to stressMultiplier and partially offset by insulin's x3 in the EGP balance. **P**: protein in the meal (g); **proteinStomach**: ingested protein in the stomach (g); **proteinGut**: protein in the small intestine; proteolysis-rate-limited absorption (τProtAbs ≈ 90 min); **AA = aminoAcidsBlood**: circulating amino-acid pool (g-equivalents); cleared with t½ ≈ 60 min; **Hill response**: PROTEIN_GLUCAGON_MAX (= 0.25) · AA^n / (AA_EC50^n + AA^n) with n = 2, EC50 = 8 g; **glycogenReserve (R)**: 0–1 fraction of liver glycogen pool; **effectiveProteinGlucagon**: hill × (0.5 + 0.5·R) — gluconeogenesis half always present; **stressMultiplier**: unified driver of EGP (glycogenBaseline + GNG + acute + chronic + dawn + protein); EGP = EGP₀ · max(0, stressMultiplier − x3). Calibration: 75 g protein → +1.6–1.7 mmol/L peak BG at 150–180 min (Paterson 2016).*
