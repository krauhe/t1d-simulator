T1D Simulator — overall model architecture (simplified)

Simplified architecture of the T1D simulator, showing the three player-controlled input pathways (food, insulin, activity) and how they connect to the Hovorka 2004 glucose-insulin core and simulator-specific extensions. Plasma insulin I acts on glucose pools Q1/Q2 via three effect compartments (x1: transport, x2: peripheral disposal, x3: EGP suppression). Stress hormones and exercise modulate hepatic glucose production and peripheral disposal, respectively. Ketogenesis is determined by plasma insulin level. The CGM sensor reads interstitial glucose with a diffusion delay. Compartments gut (D1, D2) and sc (S1/S2 + S1b/S2b) each roll up two ODE states into a single block — this is a deliberate overview-level simplification; each subsystem is detailed in its own sub-model figure.

acute + chronicstress hormone state: acute catecholamine surge and chronic hypercortisol contribution to EGP elevation

x1, x2, x3insulin effect compartments: x1 = glucose transport (Q1 to Q2), x2 = peripheral disposal, x3 = EGP suppression
EGPendogenous (hepatic) glucose production = EGP0 · max(0, stress − x3)
Q1, Q2glucose pools: Q1 = plasma glucose (BG = Q1 / VG), Q2 = peripheral (intracellular) glucose
BHBbeta-hydroxybutyrate — dominant ketone body; rate of production gated by plasma insulin level
C_intinterstitial glucose concentration read by the CGM sensor (mmol/L)
