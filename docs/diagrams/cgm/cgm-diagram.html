CGM sensor — interstitial delay, noise, drift, discontinuities (Rebrin & Steil 1999)

CGM sensor model showing the four sources of deviation between true plasma glucose G and the value cgmBG displayed to the player, following the two-compartment interstitial delay framework of Rebrin & Steil (Rebrin K, Steil GM et al., Am J Physiol 277:E561-E571, 1999). (1) Interstitial compartment C follows G with first-order kinetics at ka_int = 0.073 /min (τ ≈ 14 min), producing a low-pass lag that causes CGM to under-read during rapidly rising BG and over-read during rapid falls. (2) Gaussian sensor noise with σ ≈ 0.03 · BG mmol/L, calibrated from ~34 000 real Libre 2 readings. (3) Slow sinusoidal drift (period 4–8 h, amplitude 0.3–0.7 mmol/L) representing systematic sensor bias. (4) Rare discontinuities (~0.7 per simulated day, magnitude up to ±2 mmol/L) for compression and calibration artefacts. The signal generators noise, drift, and jump are not ODE states — they are additive corrections computed each simulation tick and summed onto C to produce cgmBG.
