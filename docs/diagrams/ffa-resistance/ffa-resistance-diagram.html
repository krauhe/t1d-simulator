Dietary fat → FFA-induced insulin resistance (Wolpert 2013)

Dietary fat absorption and the resulting delayed-phase FFA-mediated insulin resistance, based on Wolpert et al. (Wolpert 2013: 60 g fat meal → +42 % insulin requirement at 5 h post-meal). Intestinal fat simultaneously drives two outputs: (1) incremental τG via fatDelay, slowing concurrent carbohydrate absorption as fat displaces gut motility; (2) entry into plasma as free fatty acids (ffaBlood, t½ ≈ 180 min via muscle oxidation and hepatic re-esterification), which activates a Hill-saturation resistance function that divides the effective ISF by up to 1.42. The reduced ISF rescales kb1/kb2/kb3 every simulation tick, so x1/x2/x3 (transport, peripheral disposal, and EGP suppression) all weaken proportionally — a single ISF modifier feeds back into all three Hovorka effect channels.
