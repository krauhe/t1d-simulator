*Dietary fat produces two distinct pizza-effect waves. First wave: fat in the intestine increases τG via fatDelay = 18·ln(1 + fatIntestine/10), slowing carbohydrate absorption. Second wave: absorbed fat enters plasma as free fatty acids (ffaBlood, t½ ≈ 180 min) and drives a Hill resistance that divides the effective ISF by up to 1.42 (Wolpert 2013: 60 g fat → +42 % insulin requirement). The reduced ISF rescales kb1/kb2/kb3 each tick, so x1/x2/x3 (transport, disposal, EGP suppression) all weaken together — a single ISF modifier feeds back into all three Hovorka effect channels. **F**: fat content of the meal (g); **fatStomach**: fat in the stomach (g); **fatIntestine**: fat in the small intestine (g); 1/τFatAbs absorption rate (τFatAbs ≈ 150 min); **ffaBlood**: dietary free-fatty-acid plasma pool (g-eq); separate from the lipolysis pool used in ketogenesis; **FFA_CLEARANCE_HALF** = 180 min (muscle oxidation + hepatic re-esterification); **Hill resistance**: FFA_RESIST_MAX (= 0.42) · FFAⁿ / (EC50ⁿ + FFAⁿ); n = 2, EC50 = 8 g; **ffaResistanceFactor** = 1 + Hill response (range 1.0–1.42); currentISF = (baseISF · circadianISF · vasodilatation · postExBoost) / (insulinResist · ffaResist); **kb1, kb2, kb3**: Hovorka build-up rate constants — rescaled every tick by the ISF modifier; **fatDelay** (first wave): τG increase from intestinal fat — slows carb absorption immediately.*
