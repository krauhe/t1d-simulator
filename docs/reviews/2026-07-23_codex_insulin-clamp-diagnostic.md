# Insulin-clamp diagnostic after the exercise review

**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Scope:** Sustained insulin action at euglycaemia and its implications for the
exercise model
**Status:** ⚠️ PARTIAL — the discrepancy is reproduced and localized, but no
joint bolus/clamp recalibration has been performed.

## Plain-language result

The resistance-exercise model is not the source of the clamp discrepancy.
The game uses one shared exercise physiology across three weight-based
profiles. Resistance exercise is now calibrated to produce a small,
intensity-graded rise from steady state, so the possible hepatic phenomenon is
visible without individualized responder types.

The newly identified discrepancy concerns prolonged high circulating insulin:
the reference model removes glucose too quickly when insulin is held high for
hours. This can affect exercise performed with substantial active insulin and
the delayed insulin-sensitivity phase after exercise. It does not invalidate the
Young et al. acute resistance flux calibration, which was based on the
exercise-specific liver and contraction fluxes.

## [OK] Acute resistance-exercise mechanism

**Files:** `js/physiology-engine.js`, `js/hovorka.js`,
`tests/simulation.test.js`
**Subsystem:** Exercise-specific hepatic output and contraction uptake
**Evidence:**

- Shared 60-min resistance changes at 70 kg are +0.26, +0.54 and
  +0.94 mmol/L at low, medium and high intensity. The three weight profiles
  remain within 0.05 mmol/L of each other.
- At medium intensity, 45-min exercise-specific EGP AUC is
  +1.35 mmol/L and contraction uptake AUC is +1.23 mmol/L. Young et al.'s
  corresponding targets are +1.04 and +1.26 mmol/L.
- Fasted morning resistance produces +0.76 mmol/L, while a fed afternoon
  context produces -0.91 mmol/L. This reproduces the observed context-dependent
  direction reversal.

**Interpretation:** The shared model deliberately presents a mild educational
rise while retaining context-dependent reversal. It is not an individual
response prediction.

## [ADVARSEL] Sustained insulin disposal exceeds the external clamp target

**Files:** `js/hovorka.js`, `tests/insulin-clamp-validation.js`
**Subsystem:** Hovorka x1/x2 muscle insulin action
**External evidence:** Shetty et al. report pooled resting GIR
4.4 ± 0.4 mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ before exercise. The protocol targeted plasma insulin
300-550 pmol/L, approximately 50-92 mU/L.

For the reference ISF 3.0 profile, the analytical steady-state model gives:

| Plasma insulin | Model resting GIR |
|---:|---:|
| 50 mU/L | 11.90 mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ |
| 60 mU/L | 14.50 mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ |
| 92 mU/L | 20.85 mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ |

At 60 mU/L, hepatic production is already completely suppressed. The excess is
therefore generated by peripheral disposal, not by the clamp glucose controller
or by exercise.

**STATUS:** ❌ OPEN

## [ADVARSEL] One ISF scale serves two different response regimes

**Files:** `js/physiology-engine.js:235-240`, `js/hovorka.js:83-149`
**Subsystem:** Mapping from player ISF to Hovorka insulin sensitivities
**Diagnosis:** The same multiplier (`ISF / 3.75`) scales the muscle parameters
used for both:

1. a transient subcutaneous bolus, where the existing 1-unit/ISF regression
   passes; and
2. a prolonged high-insulin clamp, where glucose disposal is substantially too
   high.

With the current equation, matching 4.4 mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ at 60 mU/L would require a
model ISF near 1.22 mmol/L/U. That number is diagnostic only. Substituting it
would break the displayed ISF contract and is not an acceptable fix.

**STATUS:** ❌ OPEN

## Safe route to calibration

Do not reduce all insulin-sensitivity parameters directly. A safe calibration
must fit several contracts simultaneously:

1. transient 1-U bolus response near the selected ISF;
2. bolus onset, peak and duration;
3. basal steady state and basal plasma insulin;
4. sustained GIR across the Shetty insulin interval;
5. tissue ordering and saturation of liver versus muscle action;
6. meal-bolus and overdose regression scenarios;
7. active-insulin exercise and post-exercise sensitivity holdouts.

The likely structural solution is to separate individual ISF amplitude from the
shape/maximum of sustained muscle disposal. Candidate parameters should first
be fitted in an isolated branch or laboratory script, then tested against
independent bolus and exercise holdouts before changing the game model.
