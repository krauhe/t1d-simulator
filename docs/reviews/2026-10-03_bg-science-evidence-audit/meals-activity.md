## Prioritised findings

### MA01 — A banana ileostomy study cannot support generic 95–100% carbohydrate bioavailability

**STATUS: ✅ FIKSET (2026-10-03, local; not committed).** The generic portal-bioavailability row was replaced by a ripeness-dependent terminal-ileal endpoint, and the wrong PMID was removed. A correct PMID is given as text; the coordinating reviewer is handling final external-link validation.

- **BG locator and short quote:** §5, line 449: “Bioavailability of digestible CHO (sugar + starch) | ~95–100% to portal blood”, attributed to Englyst and Cummings (1986); line 565 still labels its numbers abstract-only and the full text unavailable.
- **Judgement:** **High; contradicted as attribution/generalisation, with a wrong linked identity.** The original measured recovery of banana carbohydrate in ileostomy effluent, not fractional appearance of all digestible carbohydrate in portal blood. Among three ileostomists, the six banana batches had approximately 46.1 ± 1.5 g total carbohydrate per 200 g fruit; across ripeness, an average 69 ± 9% of ingested banana starch was recovered as starch plus derivative oligosaccharides, and the three highest-starch batches yielded 76%, 80% and 91% recovery. Free sugars were absorbed well. These observations directly demonstrate strong food/preparation dependence, not a population-wide 95–100% portal-bioavailability constant. The study's ileostomy population and terminal-ileal endpoint are additional limits. Moreover, the linked PMID **3789750** is a different paper, Ito et al. on carcinogenic risk of environmental chemicals; Englyst and Cummings is PMID **3014853**, DOI `10.1093/ajcn/44.1.42`. Both identities were checked against Europe PMC's live bibliographic records from the local PC.
- **Original check:** `Englyst_1986_BananaCarbohydrateDigestion.pdf`, SHA-256 `4f6efaa966a567409cf692cf3024ea44bacf42bf437acba8003dc3b355eb319d`; PDF p. 5 / printed p. 46, Table 2 and adjacent “Carbohydrate recovery in effluent” paragraph, visually checked. PDF p. 1 abstract and p. 6 discussion were used for identity and the authors' interpretation. Extraction warning: table-cell loss; the original page, not the extracted table, controls this finding.
- **Correction proposal, not implementation:** Correct the wrong PMID/link in lines 449 and 565. Remove the 95–100% generic row unless a directly applicable tracer/ileal-balance source establishes the target and denominator. Replace with a conditional distinction among available carbohydrate, small-bowel digestion, portal appearance and systemic appearance; retain Englyst as an example of resistant starch varying with banana ripeness. Remove the stale abstract-only/full-text-unavailable note.

### MA03 — Protein-induced T1D hepatic output is more inferential than the wording implies

**STATUS: ✅ FIKSET (2026-10-03, local; not committed).** The 2–5 h “unopposed hepatic output” inference was replaced by a possible EGP contribution with the direct T1D flux gap stated explicitly.

- **BG locator and short quote:** §6, line 624: “The result is unopposed hepatic glucose output ... over 2–5 h.” Line 669 more cautiously says the mechanisms are unpartitioned.
- **Judgement:** **Medium; partial support, mechanistic overstatement.** Dao's review documents preserved amino-acid-stimulated glucagon in T1D, but explicitly says that few T1D studies directly quantify protein-induced endogenous glucose production (EGP). Its quantitative EGP examples after whey are from people **without** diabetes: about 25% after repeated whey doses and about 20% after a 25 g dose. T1D protein-meal glucose curves cannot establish an “unopposed” hepatic flux or its 2–5 h duration without a T1D tracer protocol. The current opening and line 669 already recognize this measurement boundary; line 624 does not.
- **Original check:** Dao PDF, SHA-256 above, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 512, “Protein Ingestion and EGP” and adjacent “Kinetics” columns, visually checked; the T1D evidence is identified there as indirect. The 20–25% values should not be moved into a T1D parameter table without their primary healthy-participant studies.
- **Correction proposal:** State that preserved glucagon plus amino-acid supply *could* increase EGP in T1D, while direct T1D flux magnitude and pathway partition remain insufficiently measured. Keep the independently observed delayed concentration rise distinct from its proposed cause.

### MA04 — Søeborg is now readable, but its oligomer thresholds are formulation-specific model inputs

**STATUS: ✅ FIKSET (2026-10-03, local; not committed).** The obsolete acquisition caveat was removed; the association model is now scoped to its formulation- and experiment-dependent inputs, without importing numerical thresholds as universal constants.

- **BG locator and short quote:** §7, line 720: “The exact concentration cut-offs and oligomer-specific absorption times attributed to Søeborg et al. are not retained because the full paper was not acquired.”
- **Judgement:** **Medium; stale access caveat, with a useful modelling boundary.** The complete article is now in the archive. Its p. 2 original describes zinc-free soluble insulin as a concentration-dependent monomer/dimer/hexamer mixture and cites underlying experiments with 50%, 75% and 100% hexamer at 33, 167 and 250 U/mL. Below approximately 5 U/mL, the paper describes predominantly smaller association states or monomer-like absorption. However, the article's own model relies on literature equilibrium constants that differ by formulation, pH, temperature and experimental method; only one listed equilibrium study was at 37 °C. These are not universal in-vivo thresholds for lispro/aspart/glulisine or directly measured T1D depot time constants.
- **Original check:** `Soeborg_2009_InsulinSubcutaneousAbsorption.pdf`, SHA-256 `0405a316b185855567e6a1630477910fee8bd503f2c60365f0cfca867b233285`; PDF p. 2 / journal p. 79, §2.1 and Table 1 (including temperature, insulin species and methods), visually checked. Conversion warns that equations and tables need original-image verification; no equation parameters were transcribed for this audit.
- **Correction proposal:** Update only the acquisition-status sentence if the chapter is revised. A concise formulation-specific concentration example could be added with its zinc-free-human-insulin and *in vitro/model* caveats, but do not promote these values to analogue-general absorption constants.

### MA06 — A controlled eccentric-versus-concentric experiment strengthens §10 beyond Asp alone

**STATUS: ✅ FIKSET (2026-10-03, local; not committed).** The Kirwan healthy-adult 48 h clamp contrast was added with its population and mediation limits; no T1D insulin-dose factor was inferred.

- **BG locator and short quote:** §10, lines 1018 and 1046: “small experiments in people without diabetes” and no fixed T1D time course. This boundary is correct but the section omits a directly pertinent comparator experiment.
- **Judgement:** **Medium; worthwhile new evidence, not a contradiction.** Kirwan et al. (1992) tested the same six healthy untrained adults after 30 min downhill running, cycle ergometry and no exercise, with clamps 48 h later. Insulin-mediated disposal after eccentric exercise was 3.47 ± 0.51 versus 5.55 ± 0.94 and 5.48 ± 1.0 mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ after concentric exercise and rest, respectively; hepatic glucose output did not differ significantly. The original identifies the uncertainty as mean ± SE. This isolates a muscle-disposal phenotype more directly than an observational cytokine rise, but cannot establish a T1D insulin-dose multiplier or a specific cytokine mediator.
- **Original check:** `Kirwan_1992_EccentricExerciseInsulinResistance.pdf`, SHA-256 `cfda89e4332a1c779bfa5d8892cf91faf5b4be2038363560f0a8c8bce2684fcb`; PDF p. 1 / printed p. 2197 abstract, protocol and units visually checked. The complete article is available for deeper methods appraisal if a rewrite proceeds.
- **Correction proposal:** Add the controlled comparator to §10's quantitative evidence, explicitly as healthy-adult 48 h clamp data. Avoid assigning the effect to IL-6 or extrapolating its percentage to all exercise/T1D.

### MA07 — Marathon data show that glycogen depletion does not guarantee greater next-day insulin action

**STATUS: ✅ FIKSET (2026-10-03, local; not committed).** The Tuominen next-morning marathon clamp contrast was added to §10 with a §8 cross-reference and no cytokine-causality claim.

- **BG locator and short quote:** §8, line 840: glycogen depletion is called a “permissive condition”; §10, lines 1026 and 1048 discuss post-race cytokines and possible glucose-disposal impairment without a human post-marathon clamp example.
- **Judgement:** **Medium; new mechanistic counterexample.** Tuominen et al. (1996) examined 19 male runners the morning after a 42 km marathon. Despite muscle glycogen being 37% lower and glycogen-synthase fractional activity 56% higher, clamp whole-body glucose disposal was 12% lower; glucose oxidation was 36% lower under the clamp while lipid oxidation rose. This supports §8's existing caveat that glycogen depletion is not sufficient to determine net insulin action. It also broadens §10 beyond a damage/cytokine explanation: substrate selection and oxidation can oppose glycogen-driven uptake, with no evidence from this study that IL-6 was the mediator. The sample lacked T1D and the exposure was an unusually prolonged race.
- **Original check:** `Tuominen_1996_PostmarathonInsulinResistance.pdf`, SHA-256 `28ae08d1a364e162b6355700d2c2971d7b87efe6907b3793908526d81727b65d`; PDF p. 1 / printed p. E336, abstract and study context visually checked.
- **Correction proposal:** Add this as a contrasting recovery protocol in §10, cross-referencing §8. Do not describe a generic post-exercise “resistance phase” or infer a cytokine/insulin-requirement curve from it.
